# Shot settings

One file per lightbox shot that goes through `tools/photo/clean_lightbox.py` (`docs/brief/07-photography.md`). They hold what the tool can't guess. Coordinates are pixels in the original after EXIF orientation.

| Key | What |
|---|---|
| `shot`, `frame`, `angle` | `IMG_3596`, `F08`, `A2` (angle codes in `07-photography.md`) |
| `variant` | Optional: a second image from the same shot (F04's hero is its front shot on a wider stage), written to `work/out/<shot>-<variant>/`; its settings file is `<shot>-<variant>.json` |
| `region` | `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`: the part of the original to work on, frame plus its shadow and some tent |
| `tent_fill` | Optional, `rows`: carry the tent's structure behind the frame row by row (front and side views, where the horizon runs across the picture and shows through a light lens) instead of filling it in from all round |
| `lenses` | One entry per lens, far lenses too (defocused or cut by the edge): `points`, one or more `[x, y]` inside it, for SAM. `exclude`, points that are not lens, for SAM to leave out (a rim as dark as the lens, seen from behind); `convex: true` for a lens with no concave edge (round, square, octagon; never F06's shield): the mask becomes its convex hull, so a reflection or a temple seen through the lens can't bite into it; `add`, polygons added to the mask by hand where SAM leaves out a part that opens onto the rim, which no filling can close (a dark temple seen through the top of F06's gradient shield) |
| `marks` | One entry per old mark: `poly` (corners), `kind` (`dark` engraving or print, `light` for a ring-light hotspot or a light print), `stroke_px`, `threshold`, `grow_px` (how far past the detected strokes to rebuild; an engraving's soft shoulder goes too), `fill_whole` (rebuild the whole polygon rather than only the detected strokes), and on a temple `temple.top` / `temple.bottom` as `[a, b]` for the silhouette's edge lines `y = a·x + b`, the guides the patch is rebuilt along, row by row; where the stripes inside the temple are not parallel to its edges (F08's metal core, seen through clear acetate, runs at a slant), `temple.guides` lists every stripe's edge from top to bottom instead, each measured on both sides of the mark, with a third coefficient for a line that bends in the picture (`y = a·x + b + c·x²`). On a textured face (gloss acetate that mirrors the tent's dimpled wall, a grained core) the rebuilt rows give only the smooth shading, and the texture comes from the same rows where the face is clean: `texture_dx` px along the picture (negative: toward the left), or `texture_from`, segments `[x0, x1, s0, s1]` (columns x0 to x1 take columns s0 to s1; overlapping segments cross-fade) for a patch longer than the clean face beside it. Keep the polygon a few pixels inside the temple's edges and below its lit top face: the stroke detector takes an edge for a stroke. On a patterned face (F01's tortoise) rows rebuilt from their ends streak the pattern: `fill: "fsr"` rebuilds the strokes by frequency-selective reconstruction instead (OpenCV contrib), which carries the pattern through them. A mark seen through a lens (a print on the far temple's inner face) comes off before the lens is measured: defocused, it takes the smoothest surface through its border (`fill: "smooth"`, Laplace's equation, grain added back); where the band it sits on shows texture, the band is carried on from its clean end only (`side`: `left` or `right`, with `texture_from`). Rows rebuilt from both ends took the rim's black in, or streaked |
| `lens_cleanup` | Optional: `tophat_px`, `threshold` (reflections); `dust_px`, `dust_threshold`, `dust_min` (dust); `paint`, polygons of reflections too big to detect, refilled as clean lens; `keep`, polygons of the lens's own structure that would pass for dust or a reflection, left as they are; `smooth`, `flat` (one tint per lens) or `gradient` (gradient lenses), which also drops whatever is seen through the lens; with `keep_behind: true` (light lenses: F01, F03) what is clearly darker than the lens's own tint, a temple or nose pad behind it, stays, and only the tent behind is evened out. Light lenses also want a higher `threshold` (0.2): their texture would pass for reflections. The tint is always the one measured in the shot itself. `as_shot: true` (a lens seen at a steep angle, F06's three-quarter and detail, which shows mostly its own edge and reflections) keeps the lens exactly as photographed: its transmission is taken against the stage color, not the tent behind it, and its own edge (the bright facet along it, the dark line where it turns) is not taken for reflections or dust |
| `gcd` | `view` (the Blender render's turn of the face it sits on, e.g. `yaw72`, `yaw-16`, positive when the face recedes to the right; with a tilt, `yaw-57p31`, positive when seen from above), `relief_mm`, `length_mm` (from `06-products.md`), `soften_px` (match the focus), and one of three placements. On a temple: `hinge_top` / `hinge_bottom` (the hinge line at the temple's top and bottom edge), `temple_mm` (face height there), `axis` (unit vector along the temple, away from the hinge), `px_per_mm_along`. Measured directly (F02's wing): `center`, `axis`, `down`, `px_per_mm_along`, `px_per_mm_down`. In perspective: `anchors_px` with `center`, `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` (on a temple, from `tools/photo/temple_gcd.py`, below; `measured_with` and `measured` record how) |
| `framing` | Optional. Each angle has one rule, in `content/photography/framing.json`; keys here override it for the shot (`aspect`, `width`, `height`, `bottom`, `center_y`). `box` `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` keeps the photographer's crop instead (the A6 close-ups at 4:5; the landscape heroes at their full width, 3:2); it may reach past the photograph, padded with the stage color, and a frame running off the photograph inside it keeps its shadow to the edge. `cut`, e.g. `{"right": 3060}`, ends the picture sooner on a side where the frame can't be held beyond (a blur the segmentation loses): the canvas ends there as at the photograph's edge |
| `alt` | Optional: which view phrase of the alt text (`alt.angles` in `content/copy/en.json`) the picture takes when its angle's own doesn't describe it: `A1` or `A2` for a hero shot front on or at three-quarters, `A3-left` or `A3-rear` for a side view taken as a low three-quarter from the left or from behind, `A6-front` (F02's emblem on the front's corner), `A6-corner` (F04's and F05's, where the temple meets the front), `A6-hinge` (F08's detail, which shows no emblem). `tools/photo/export_web.py` writes the alt text into `assets/photography/MANIFEST.csv` |
| `shadow_zone` | Optional, where the frame's own shadow can fall: `reach`, `side`, `soft` in px (below the frame in the picture); `around` in px for a view from straight above, where the shadow lies all round the frame |

## Placing F02's GCD

F02's GCD replaces the two pin heads on the right wing. The approved front shot (IMG_3423) fixes it: centered between the pins, 8.8 mm long, the pins 2.64 mm apart and 1.68 mm across. On any other angle, measure the pins: their midpoint is the center, the line through them the axis, their spacing gives `px_per_mm_along` and their height `px_per_mm_down`, and the ratio of the two gives the yaw (`cos yaw = along / down`). Where the wing is too blurred to show the pins, they sit about 39% and 61% across the wing's flat face and 53% down.

## Placing a temple GCD

Measure it with `tools/photo/temple_gcd.py` (its docstring has the method) and paste its `gcd` block in; keep the command in `gcd.measured_with` so it can be re-run. Every input is measured from the picture (column and row profiles, local contrast), never read off by eye:

- `--cols x0:x1:step` over a long straight stretch of the temple (a temple that bends in the picture fits badly: the tool reports the fit in `edges.fit_px`, keep it under about 2 px, and how much the temple's height changes across the stretch in `edges.height_change_px`), `--band y0:y1` around it. Where another temple touches this one in the picture, measure the two edge lines by hand and pass `--edges m,c m,c` instead.
- How the outer face starts below the temple's silhouette: `--face-dark 40` on black acetate seen a little from above (70 on brighter gloss), `--face-light 22` seen from high above (a dark top face, a lit outer face), neither when no top face shows.
- `--hinge x,y x,y`: two points on the seam where the temple's face meets the end-piece (a thin dark gap with a highlight on one side). It is not the front's edge: F03's end-piece runs about 7 mm on from the seam.
- `--seam-mm`: how far the seam runs along the temple from the temple's top edge to its bottom edge, on the side drawing (where the temple's edge lines start): F01 1.9, F03 0, F06 0.97, F09 3.19.
- `--temple-mm`, the temple's height at the hinge on the side drawing (`06-products.md`; F06 measures 9.65 between the drawing's edge lines) and `--face-mm`, the real height between the two edges measured (the temple height less about 0.5 mm when the top face shows).
- `--print` with the four corners of the old print's first line (DARKEST: top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left, as read), from its strokes: the letters' ends are easy to miss by eye (the F06 hero's first placement missed two letters).

Which model: per frame, the shot whose 3D model is best founded (a long stretch over which the temple's height clearly changes, and an old print that comes out with its usual proportions) gets the 3D model (`--model 3d` when the edges meet at under 2° but the stretch is long), and its `print_check.ends_mm` is the frame's ruler (F01 26.07,42.5 from IMG_3556; F03 23.66,38.43 from IMG_3495; F06 26.26,41.42 from IMG_3483). A profile that shows the temple square on (its height the same along its length) gives the print's distances directly, against the temple's height: F09 24.05,39.9 from the left profile IMG_3402, which also shows the hinge line's slant (11°, 12° on the drawing). Every other temple shot of the frame passes it as `--print-mm d1,d2` (`--model ruler`), or only the far end, `--print-mm d2`, when the print's near end is too faint to place.
