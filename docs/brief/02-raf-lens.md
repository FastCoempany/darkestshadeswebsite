# 02 · The Raf lens (internal)

Raf Simons is the design lens for this site, and he stays internal (`00-decisions.md` #5). Nothing here ships: no name, no likeness, no quotes, no references to his collections or his collaborators' artwork. What ships is the way of working.

The founder's line for it: **philosophy as manifesto, before product.** Prada is the idol for the house standard: restraint, intelligence, the price on the page.

## What it means on this site

1. **A position before a product.** Every page opens with a statement before it shows anything for sale. The home page is a manifesto first and a store second. A product page opens on the frame and one line, then the facts.
2. **One idea per screen.** One statement, one image, or one action. If a screen needs two headlines, it is two screens.
3. **Type is the image.** The manifesto is set huge in Owners Wide and does the work a campaign photo would. No decorative graphics behind it.
4. **Real people with a point of view.** The seven models read as individuals, not mannequins: a defined face and build, an attitude, plain uniform-like clothes. Casting is content.
5. **An archive, not a catalog.** Everything is numbered and documented like a collection in a museum: Drop 001, Look 01, F01, the technical drawings, the emblem's spec. Frames are presented like pieces, with their measurements.
6. **Stillness, then one cut.** The site holds very still and makes one decisive move per page (a cut to full-bleed, a scale jump), never a carousel of small motions. See `03-references.md`.
7. **Youth and subculture as substance.** The Brand Story's community (DJs, filmmakers, digital artists, designers) shows up in who is cast and what they're doing, never as slang in the copy.
8. **Honest.** The price is shown. Quantities and dates are real. Nothing pretends to be scarcer, older or more popular than it is.

## What it is not
- Not logo-mania. The GCD appears at most once per screen.
- Not streetwear hype: no "fire", no "must-have", no all-caps exclamations, no fake drop timers.
- Not a Prada imitation: no copying of Prada's layouts, wordmark style, triangle shapes, or campaign images.
- Not a fashion-week pastiche: no runway photos, no designer names, no "as seen at".

## Tests for every screen
- Does it state a position before it sells?
- Is there exactly one thing to look at?
- Would it still say something with the product removed?
- Is every number on it true today?
- Could this screen belong to any other sunglasses brand? If yes, redo it.
