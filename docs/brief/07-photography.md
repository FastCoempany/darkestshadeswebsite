## Track 1: product (lightbox → catalog)

### The source set
One iPhone session (Apr 15 2025): 317 JPGs, 248 portrait and 69 landscape, at three resolutions, on a light seamless in a light tent with a ring light. Shot group per frame in `06-products.md`. The ring light leaves hotspots on gloss acetate and lenses; some frames show the tent floor edge or the room. `content/photography/lightbox-angle-set.json` holds the first-pass picks per frame and angle (picked from thumbnails; confirmed at full resolution as each frame is cleaned), alternates, rejects, gaps and the four shots that look misfiled.

**Which side.** The GCD is on the wearer's right only, so the three-quarter, the GCD detail and the hero use right-side shots wherever the set has them (re-picked Oct 19 2026); the side view keeps a true left profile where the set has no right one (F01, F03, F05, F09). `lightbox-angle-set.json` records the side of each pick. Seen from the front, the wearer's right is on the image's left. On an outside view, the front at the image's right with the temple running left is the right side; a view from behind flips that, so those are told by the hinge hardware (it sits on the inside) and by the old print reading the right way round (it sits on the outside). F08 was never shot from its right side outside (`00-decisions.md` #34: its images show no GCD).

### Pipeline (per selected shot)
`tools/photo/clean_lightbox.py` does steps 2–10 and has cleaned all seven angles of all eight frames (56 shots). Each shot has a settings file, `content/photography/shots/<shot>.json`, with the measurements the tool can't guess: the working region, a point inside each lens, a polygon per old mark with the temple's top and bottom edge lines, the hinge line and temple axis for the GCD, and the framing.

```
python3 tools/photo/zipget.py --picks content/photography/lightbox-angle-set.json --frames F08
python3 tools/photo/clean_lightbox.py content/photography/shots/IMG_3596.json [--debug]
```

1. **Pull** the original from the release zip by byte range (no 1.5 GB download): `tools/photo/zipget.py`.
2. **Load**: EXIF orientation, Display P3 → sRGB from the embedded profile; all the math below runs in linear light.
3. **Segment**: a soft alpha for rims and temples from ISNet (rembg); each lens from SAM with the shot's point prompts (each model runs in its own process: together they need more memory than the workspace gives one process). A lens mask gets its holes filled and small notches closed (what SAM leaves out is something seen through the lens), and a lens with no concave edge can be made convex (`"convex": true`). Masks are cached in `work/out/<shot>/masks/`.
4. **Model the tent**: a robust smooth fit, plus the tent's own structure the fit misses (the sweep's horizon, light falloff), measured right up to the frame wherever its shadow can't fall and carried under it.
5. **Keep the frame's own shadow**: image ÷ tent inside the shadow zone (below the frame and a little to the sides, since the tent is lit from above and the camera looks down), fading out by 450 px. Seen from straight above (A4), the shadow lies all round the frame (`shadow_zone.around`). Beside the frame the zone fades in slowly from the top, so it never cuts a shadow along a straight line; where the frame reaches almost to the picture's sides (the F08 hero's end-pieces) it has no sides at all (`shadow_zone.side: 0`): there it took in all the tent beside the frame, the tent model came in from the far end of each row, and the tent showed as a grey block below a straight line. Only darkening that joins the frame is shadow: a speck on the tent's fabric inside the zone is not (it sat on the F06 detail's stage as a smudge). Everywhere else the new stage is one even color.
6. **See-through lenses**: transmission per color channel = image ÷ tent, so a lens is a color filter (F01's blue and F03's yellow keep their color on any background). Ring-light reflections (bright top-hat spots) and dust (small dips, deeper than the noise in absolute and relative terms) are painted out of the transmission. No solid-filled lenses; transmission is measured, not set (median: F08 about 12–14%, F09 about 6–7%, F02 about 3%, a little higher in blue). Where what the lens sees (the tent's horizon, a nose pad, a temple behind it) would come out in fragments, each lens is refilled with its own measured tint (`smooth`: `flat`, or `gradient` for gradient lenses), its grain put back in the brightness only, as the camera has it (drawn per color channel, it showed as colored speckle on the dark lenses); on a light lens (F01, F03) what is clearly darker than the tint, a temple behind it, stays (`keep_behind`), right up to the rim, and a speck or reflection on it goes into what is kept round it (filled with lens tint, specks on the temple seen through F01's lens came out as pale dots). Every shot keeps the lens as its own photograph shows it: the yellow, the blue and the greys are measured in that shot, never set or carried over from another angle (founder, Oct 19 2026). One exception: F04's close-up, whose lenses, seen at a steep angle with the tent's light on them, measure about three times lighter than F04's dark grey; they take the front view's measured tint (`lens_cleanup.tint`, `00-decisions.md` #38), and their edge is the convex mask's line, feathered outward as soft as the photograph's out-of-focus edge (cut square, the dark lens ended in a 1-2 px step; read against the lighter, textured lens in the picture, the tent's dimples by the rim stayed as bright crumbs on the dark lens and its blurred bottom went ragged). Seen at a steep angle (F06's three-quarter and detail), a lens shows mostly its own edge and reflections, a band along the rim among them; it is kept exactly as shot (`as_shot`): no refill, which stopped short of the rim with a ragged edge, and its transmission taken against the stage color rather than the tent behind it, which made it lighter than the frame round it, with a ragged step where SAM's mask stopped inside the lens. A lens runs right up to the solid rim (a light lens's last few pixels otherwise showed as a pale line of bare stage, F01). Where lens and frame meet, the masks draw a line in whole pixels (SAM's for the lens, the model's alpha for the rim, which calls a dark lens solid), so within a few pixels of it each pixel's opacity is read from the picture: how far it lies from the lens's color just inside towards the frame's just outside. The edge then follows the photograph, without a staircase (F03's yellow), and a dark lens keeps no band of its old self along the rim (F04's front); a highlight along the rim stays frame, and the rim's soft edge lies over the lens, not bare stage; where SAM's mask runs past the lens into a dark rim (F05's detail, F08's three-quarter, the bottom of F03's yellow lens in the front view), the lens's color is read further in and the rim taken back out of the mask; the lens's color is never read from what a light lens keeps behind it (a temple just inside the rim made the strip of lens between them read as a highlight on the rim, a grey dash under F01's front rim); the lens's own edge (the rim's dark inner edge just inside the hole, the bright facet along a lens seen at a steep angle) is never taken for dust or a reflection, which filled it in as a dark block or a notch; the frame's color is weighted between its band's darker and lighter halves, not switched between them, which made blocks; each pixel's reading is evened out along the edge with its neighbors at the same depth, so a dark line along a lens's edge doesn't come and go in dashes (F02's and F04's details), and where lens and rim are too alike to tell pixel by pixel, the picture is read again smoothed over a few pixels before the masks' line is used; then the edge, measured point by point along the lens's outline as its offset from SAM's line, is smoothed along it (a running median over about 80 px, from the points where the reading could tell lens from frame, carried across the stretches where it could not), and each pixel's opacity is held within a pixel or two of that line, more where the edge is soft: read pixel by pixel, the edge went wherever each pixel's reading took it, in crumbs where the texture seen through a lens met the rim (F02's and F04's details), in a step where the reading lapsed and the masks' line came back (F04's front), in a blob where a dark reflection touched the rim (F08's front); on a light lens that keeps what is behind it, not beside a band seen through it that runs into the rim (where a temple seen through the lens starts at the rim, it starts there: held to the smoothed line, its first pixels went and the rest floated free, F01's three-quarter; a dark lens drops what is behind it, and held there its edge showed the masks' line as a nick, F08's three-quarter); what that gives back to the frame takes the frame's color beside it, and no more than the frame's own opacity there (a dark grey rim the model calls a little see-through stays so: given back whole, a strip of it went black along the F09 hero's lens), what it gives to the lens the cleaned lens; a dark band seen through the lens that runs into the rim (a temple behind it) reaches more than 22 px into the lens and is lens, not rim, near where it runs that deep, while a defocused rim's blur inside an overshooting mask reaches about 16 and stays rim (taken for such a band, F04's and F05's details lost their soft edges to a hard line along the mask); the lens's part of an edge pixel is the cleaned lens, unless that is much darker than the lens beside it (a light lens keeps what is darker behind it, and the rim's blur kept so showed as a dark line along the F03 hero's yellow); a lens kept as shot is not read (its edge is the photograph's already); a speck of SAM's mask outside the lens is dropped before the convex hull, which stretched to it across the rim (F01's inside view and detail); a part SAM leaves out that opens onto the rim is added by hand (`add`: the temple seen through the top of F06's shield).
7. **Remove the old marks**: per-shot polygons (from `darkestshades/brand/lightbox/onyx-emblem/process/cfgs.py` where they exist). On a temple, the face is straightened between its measured top and bottom edges and rebuilt row by row, so the acetate, the core wire and the bevel continue through the patch; grain is added back. Where the stripes inside a temple are not parallel to its edges (F08's grained metal core runs at a slant inside the clear acetate), the rows follow every stripe's own measured edge instead (`temple.guides`), or the core comes out doubled. A textured face (gloss acetate that mirrors the tent's dimpled wall on the F06 hero, F08's grained core) would come out streaked: there the rebuilt rows give only the smooth shading, and the texture comes from the clean face further along the same temple (`texture_dx`, `texture_from`). On F01's blue tortoise the rows streaked the pattern, so its lettering comes off by frequency-selective reconstruction (`fill: "fsr"`, OpenCV contrib), which carries the tortoise through the strokes; the strokes are found at a firm threshold and followed into their fainter parts from there (`weak_threshold`): one loose threshold took the tortoise's mottling too and smeared a third of the face into a blue haze, one firm one left the letters' shallow ends as dark dots in a row (F01's detail). Old prints show in places the first pass missed: on the inner face of F01's far temple, seen through the lens (the marks now come off before the lens is measured: in the three-quarter view the print is defocused and runs into the rim's blur, so it takes the smoothest surface through its border, `fill: "smooth"`; in the side view the band shows texture, so it is carried on from the print's clean end, `side`; rows rebuilt from both ends took the rim's black in, or streaked), and engraved on the top of F08's front ends (its top and three-quarter views). Gloss highlights on acetate stay as shot; a ring-light hotspot on acetate, where a shot has one, is listed as a mark with `"kind": "light"`.
8. **Place the GCD**, once, on the wearer's right (`00-decisions.md` #31): shots of the left side get the old marks removed and nothing added. `tools/blender/gcd_render.py --light lightbox` renders it at the face's yaw (bright surround, the phone's dark body in the reflections, the ring light on angled views only; made on first use into `work/gcd/` with its 2D anchors), at the size in `06-products.md`, centered 0.82 × the temple height from the hinge and 39% down the face (on F02's wing: over the two pin heads; on F04's and F05's end-pieces: centered on the triangle each forms with the front's side), always reading left to right, clear of the lens bay's bevel; warped onto the measured face, with a contact occlusion and focus-matched softening. Never a flat sticker. On a temple the placement is physically measured (founder, Oct 19 2026) with `tools/photo/temple_gcd.py`, from measurements of the picture, never by eye: the temple's edges (column profiles), the hinge line (the seam where the temple's face meets the end-piece, not the front's edge: on F03 the end-piece's side runs about 7 mm on from the seam), the seam's slant across the face from the side drawing (F01 1.9 mm, F06 0.97, F09 3.19 from the temple's top to its bottom; the old print's letters stand square to the temple and lean off the seam in the picture just as much), and the old print's first line, DARKEST, located from its strokes. Per frame, the temple's face is modelled in 3D on the shot where its edges converge most: the edges are parallel on the frame, so where they meet in the picture gives the temple's direction (the focal length is in the EXIF), and the hinge line gives the face's down direction and its distance. That model also locates the old print (F01 26.1 to 42.5 mm from the hinge, F03 23.7 to 38.4, F06 26.3 to 41.4); where a profile shows the temple square on, its height the same along its length, the print's distances are read straight off it instead (F09 24.1 to 39.9, from the left profile). On the frame's other shots the print is the ruler: the hinge and the print's two ends are three points at known distances along the temple, which fixes its perspective. The print's proportions alone (its first line about 7 times as long as tall) are a last resort: its height is too small to measure well, and they drew the GCD up to a third too short. The tool writes `view` (the render's turn and tilt, e.g. `yaw-57p31`) and `anchors_px` into the shot's settings, with the command in `gcd.measured_with`. Judged by eye, the turn of a temple is easy to underestimate: the first placements on F01, F03 and the F09 hero drew the GCD 40% to 100% too long for the angle; and the hinge is easy to misplace: the first F03 hero and detail placements had the GCD on the end-piece.
9. **Re-stage and frame**: the frame over (stage `#E4E2DD` × shadow × lens transmission). No part of the frame is lighter in any channel than its share of the tent behind it allows: where it is darker than that, it is more opaque (past 0.85, the opacity is at least what keeps each channel at zero or above), so it comes out as photographed; unmixed at the lower opacity, a deep color's weak channels went below zero, were cut there and took the stage's share back on top, a grey film over F02's red tortoise, worst in the out-of-focus end pieces, and grey outlines round each patch where only the most colored parts had been made solid. A soft edge is unmixed against the tent seen just outside it, not against the model under the frame (filled in from all round, it is brighter wherever the tent darkens toward the frame), so it fades into the stage without a grey halo, a step or a ragged line; the frame's own shadow, and the blur of a part the model missed (a defocused far temple, F01's blue, F02's red), are not taken for tent. An open gap (a bridge, the space between a side view's temples) is see-through only past its soft edge, and the tent seen through it is read on its own clear part: the model filled in under the frame is brighter than that tent, which left gaps grey and their edges ragged (F08's bridge, F03's and F04's side views). Where the model calls a broad, uncolored area of tent partly frame (F03's nose bridge), the physical opacity wins, so the area follows the picture's shading. So it does over the model's own soft edge round such a gap, which it calls solid though it is the tent with the frame's blur over it (a band of the old tent's grey stood round the gap between F03's temples in the side view): grown from a gap that is clearly tent, closed in by the frame and broad (30,000 px and more in the original; the soft edge along the frame's outside, open to the stage, and round a narrow gap is the frame's real blur and stays as it is: grown there, it left light blocks and dashes along F04's and F09's outer edges and washed a hinge lighter in F03's side view), through what is lighter than solid frame and uncolored, only ever darker, at most 45 px, so a light part of the frame past it is not reached (F08's clear temples; at 150 px it took the light parts of F03's side-view hinge), fading out over its last 12 px where it runs that far (cut off square, it left a hard stepped line at the lower corner of F03's side-view gap), and a patch of grey the model took for tent beside a lit face of the frame takes nothing with it (the rim's inner wall by F03's nose bridge in the front view). A clearly colored pixel inside the frame is the frame's own color, not the tent seen through it: F01's dark blue tortoise read 95% opaque, and the 5% of new stage seen through it washed it lavender (not near black, where the camera's own color noise passes for color, block by block). A defocused part well inside the frame that the model is unsure of (F06's far hinge, round and out of focus) counts as solid, so it shows no lighter patch.
10. **Frame by angle** (`content/photography/framing.json`): one rule per angle, applied to the frame's own extent (lenses included), so an angle has the same size and the same height on the page in every frame's set. All at 4:5: the frame spans 78% of the canvas width in A1 and 84% in the others; its lowest point sits at 62% of the height in A1, 68% in A2, 65% in A3 and 69% in A5, with the contact shadow below; A4 is centred. Where a temple runs off a side of the photograph, the visible width isn't the frame's, so its height sets the scale instead (29% of the canvas height in A2, 27% in A3), unless the frame would then fill less than 75% of the width; that side stays the photograph's edge, the temple running off the picture with its shadow. The A6 close-ups keep the photographer's crop at 4:5; the photographed heroes keep the photographer's full width at 3:2, the stage carried above and below; F04's hero, which the shoot doesn't have, is its front shot on a 3:2 stage, the frame spanning 86% of the width, as in the photographed ones (85 to 92%).
11. **Export** (`tools/photo/export_web.py`): every final to `assets/photography/` as `ds-<frame>-<angle>-<width>` in AVIF, WebP and JPEG, 640, 960, 1440 and 2048 px wide as far as the master reaches (never upscaled; a close-up cropped smaller also comes at its own width), downsized in linear light, sRGB, no metadata; registered in `MANIFEST.csv` with its alt text (`content/copy/en.json`, `alt`; a shot whose angle's own phrase doesn't describe it names another with `alt` in its settings). The masters, the 16-bit cut-out (`DS-F01-A2-v01-master.png`) and the 16-bit stage version (`DS-F01-A2-v01-stage.png`), go to the DAM, `dam/product/` (#39), losslessly recompressed; a master is rewritten only when its source in `work/out/` is newer (`--no-masters` skips them).

Outputs in `work/out/<shot>/` (git-ignored; finals go to the DAM): `stage.jpg` (2000 wide) and `stage-full.png` (16-bit); `cutout.png`, one 16-bit RGBA file with the lens filter folded into the alpha (the master); `layers/frame.png`, `layers/lens.png` and `layers/shadow.png`, the exact decomposition for compositing on anything (multiply the background by shadow and lens, then the frame on top; exact in linear light, close in an ordinary sRGB document); `compare.jpg`; `report.json` (lens transmission per channel, pixels cleaned, GCD size and position).

**Resolution and vectors.** Photos stay raster at their native resolution (12 MP originals); upscaling for print uses a free super-resolution model (OpenCV's EDSR from GitHub, or Real-ESRGAN weights) and is marked as upscaled in the manifest. The infinitely scalable masters are vector: the frames' line art at true scale (`tools/drawings.py`) and the GCD (`brand/emblems/gcd/svg/`, plus its 3D model). Tracing photos into vector shapes is not used: glossy acetate traces into thousands of flat blobs.

### Done means
- No hotspot larger than 0.2% of the frame area; no old mark anywhere, including through the lens.
- Each lens as its own photograph shows it: transmission measured in that shot, never evened out across a frame (`00-decisions.md` #32; F04's close-up takes F04's front-view tint, #38); ring-light reflections and dust cleaned the same way on every shot.
- Same stage color (ΔE < 2 between any two finals), same contact shadow recipe, same framing.
- GCD at the specified size, with a believable reflection and a contact edge; checked at 200% zoom.

### Best path, and why
The generator is **Figma `generate_image` with `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`** on the Starter plan's included AI credits (2 credits an image; 500 a month, 150 a day; generation stops at the cap and nothing is charged). It is text-only, so:

1. **Generate the person and the scene together, without glasses.** One image per shot, so light and perspective agree. Eyes open, looking past the camera, nothing touching the face or temples, hair clear of the ears where a temple must sit.
2. **Pose to match the cut-outs.** The frames exist at three head angles (A1 front, A2 three-quarter, A3 side), so every prompt fixes the head angle to one of them ("head turned three-quarters to the left, chin level"). The composite then needs a small warp, not a re-render.
3. **Hold the face with the model bible plus the glasses.** The frames cover the eyes and brows, the most identity-defining part of a face, so a fixed description of hair, skin, jaw, lips and build carries identity across shots. Cast faces without glasses appear only in the casting sheets.
4. **Composite the real frame** (`tools/photo/wear.py`, settings per image in `content/photography/people/`):
   - the portrait at twice its size: the generator returns about 1.2 MP (960 × 1280 for 3:4), so the person, and only the person, is doubled with EDSR super-resolution (OpenCV contrib, in tiles to fit the workspace's memory; on a face it came closest of the free models to the original: +1.3 dB over bicubic, against +0.5 for FSRCNN, +0.3 for ESPCN, 0 for LapSRN); the frame is drawn from its 12 MP photograph at the doubled size, and the manifest marks the image upscaled;
   - face landmarks and head pose from InsightFace (`pip install insightface onnxruntime`; its models download from GitHub releases; MediaPipe's models sit on a host this workspace can't reach);
   - scale from the pupils (the person's pupillary distance: 62 mm for the women, 64 for the men, 63 for NB), then fit the photographed lens openings onto the 1:1 drawing's lens openings at that scale and the head's pose (a homography from eight points), so the frame is as big on the face as it is in life (a 45□24 frame has its lens centres 69 mm apart, so the pupils sit a little inside them); on the proof face the eight fronts came out within 2.5% of their drawings' widths;
   - the cut-out comes from the cleaned shot of the matching angle (A1 for a face to camera); in A2 and A3 the far temple is hidden behind the head and the near one runs to the ear, under hair where hair crosses it;
   - only the front is worn: the drawing's front outline, placed as the lens openings are and then fitted to the photographed front (an affine fit of the two filled shapes: the product shot sees a rim's top face from a little above, up to 2 mm of it), with a millimetre of slack, cuts what the product shot shows past the front: F03's temples, opened and seen from above, stood over the brows like horns; an end piece's side face, which the product camera saw from one side, lay along the frame's edge as a pale stripe; the tent seen soft under the bridge lay on the skin as a grey film;
   - lens layer: each lens's measured tint, flat, multiplies what's behind it; nothing the product shot saw through the lens (temples, the tent) comes along. A lens covers what the cleaned shot's lens layer covers and runs on under the rim to its solid part and a little under it, never past the frame: cut at SAM's line, the bare face showed in a hairline ring inside every rim. On the lens's front surface, a faint sheen of the room in front (lighter at the top) and one soft reflection of the key, placed for the scene's light;
   - the frame's contact shadow on the skin, offset away from the key light and softened for its size;
   - the frame lit like the scene: its color from a patch that is neutral in the scene (the backdrop, a white shirt), its level from the backdrop against the tent it was shot in, or set where the backdrop is dark. In a dark scene, the frame's own photographed highlights (the neutral part of its color: a gloss reflection has the light's color, so the tortoise and the gold stay) are kept where the surface faces the key light and dimmed to the scene's surround elsewhere, the way a rim's faces turn read from its outline; a highlight is never added or moved;
   - the GCD comes with the frame, as placed on the product shot.
5. **Grade everything together**: the SS90 look (`01-brand-system.md`): fine grain, lifted blacks, warm paper whites, faint halation, low saturation, applied to the finished composite in `wear.py` so person, scene and frame share one surface; the grain and halation scale with the picture, so they look the same at any export size. 3:4 portraits are then cut to the gallery's 4:5.
6. **Check** each final against a list: scale against the face, nose-pad contact, temple to ear, no glasses artifacts from the generator, hands and fingers plausible, no text or logos in the scene, the GCD correct and once.

Why not the alternatives: generating people *with* glasses would invent the product, which the founder's own AI-photography guide rules out; separate person and background generations never quite agree on light; local open models (SDXL, Flux) are too slow and too large for this workspace's CPU; the Gemini app holds faces from references but its free output carries a visible watermark, so it can only make references, never finals; Weave and every paid route are out (#24).
